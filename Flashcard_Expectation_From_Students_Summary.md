# What Students Actually Want From AI Flashcards


**Tools used:** Anki × 3 · Quizlet × 2 · Own notes + AI × 1

## What already works

| Respondent | Quote | Context |
|---|---|---|
| R6 (uses Anki) | "If you forgot a definition, you can immediately carry on with other flashcards before coming back to it." | most beneficial aspect |
| R5 (uses Anki daily) | "Spaced repetition and the cards letting you drill common patterns / memorisable answers." | most beneficial aspect |
| R1 (doesn't currently use flashcards) | "The brevity of the information on them." | most beneficial aspect |
| R3 (uses Quizlet) | "Quick to use, simple to make, active recall." | most beneficial aspect |

## Where today's tools fall short

- **R6** — Surface which cards get forgotten most often, instead of treating every card equally.
- **R3** — Stat tracking, and decks that sync cleanly across devices.
- **R7** — A way to type out an answer and get it checked, not just flip the card and self-grade.
- **R8** — Easier bulk import for large sets — and fewer ads interrupting a study session.
- **R2** — Nothing missing from Anki specifically — spaced repetition is why R2 picks it over Quizlet.

## The AI question (n = 9)

**Trust in an app that auto-generates cards from PDF slides** (scale 1 = wouldn't trust it at all, 5 = would trust it completely). No respondent chose 1 or 5.

| Rating | Responses |
|---|---|
| 1.5 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 4 |
| 4 | 2 |

Average: **2.8 / 5**

**If it did generate cards for you, should it…**

- Write new cards for you — 6 / 9 (67%)
- Just format text you select — 3 / 9 (33%)

A clear majority want the AI to generate, not just tidy up — but trust in that same AI averages under 3 out of 5. The requirements below explain why.

## The spec, in their words

1. **Never hallucinate an answer**
   > "Make sure the AI does not hallucinate answers." — R5

2. **Keep the course's own voice**
   > "Make sure AI does not 'rephrase' answers to be in a different style from the course." Trained on real lecture slides and good decks, "so it doesn't generate flashcards that are useless." — R5, R2

3. **Stay short**
   > "Keep cards relatively concise so that it tests core knowledge without requiring lots of time to complete." — R5

4. **Render formulae properly**
   > "Make sure they can cleanly print or display formulae and mathematical symbols." — R1

5. **Export straight into Anki**
   > "Make sure that these flashcards can be easily imported into Anki." — R5

6. **Show its edits, don't hide them**
   > "Add options for AI edits and the ability to see what the AI has modified, so you don't have to go through the whole thing to check what is valid." — R8

7. **Fill gaps, don't replace the deck**
   > Once a deck is finished, "the AI could suggest cards that may or may not be missing, and the user can choose to add / ignore / edit those suggestions." — R6

8. **Let students test themselves against it**
   > "Using AI to check solutions as well. Would make it more quiz-like and fun." — R7
